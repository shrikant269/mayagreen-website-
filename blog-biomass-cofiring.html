Biomass co-firing: India's next big fuel opportunity, and why Vidarbha matters

Maya Green · October 2026 · 5 min read

India has made biomass a required part of coal power. Coal-fired plants must now co-fire at least 5% biomass pellets, and plants in and around the National Capital Region an additional 2%, from FY 2025-26. The policy is in place. The supply is not.

A demand gap of tens of millions of tonnes

At full compliance, the co-firing mandate implies demand of roughly 50 million tonnes of pellets a year. India's pellet production today is estimated at only 2 to 3 million tonnes. That gap is one of the largest unmet fuel markets in the country.

Raw material is not the constraint. India produces an estimated 228 million tonnes of surplus crop residue every year, and a large share of it is burned in the open after harvest.

The real bottleneck: sourcing and trust

Crop residue is spread across millions of small farms, each producing a few tonnes in a short harvest window. Collecting it reliably, at a price farmers accept, with consistent quality, is hard. Buyers also increasingly want proof of where the fuel came from, for compliance, ESG reporting and carbon accounting.

Most pellet makers buy through aggregators and cannot trace a tonne back to a field. That limits both volume and value.

Why torrefied pellets

Torrefaction is a mild roasting step, typically around 250–350°C, that turns raw biomass into a darker, more coal-like fuel. Compared with ordinary white pellets, torrefied pellets carry more energy per tonne, resist moisture better in storage, and grind more like coal, which makes them easier for power plants and industrial boilers to use at higher blending ratios.

Why Vidarbha

Vidarbha is one of India's largest cotton and soybean belts, with tur widely grown alongside. Cotton stalks in particular are woody, have little use as fodder, and are commonly burned after harvest. The region also hosts major thermal power capacity and industrial boilers within trucking distance, which keeps logistics costs manageable.
