What is digital MRV, and why carbon buyers trust it

Maya Green · October 2026 · 5 min read

A carbon credit is only as good as the evidence behind it. MRV, short for monitoring, reporting and verification, is how a project proves that the emission reductions it claims actually happened. Doing it digitally, from the first kilogram, changes what buyers can trust.

Why MRV matters now

The voluntary carbon market has faced hard questions about over-crediting and weak evidence. Buyers have responded by paying more for credits with transparent data, independent verification and clear community benefit, and by avoiding credits they cannot trace.

For a biomass project, the questions are concrete. Which farm did this residue come from? Was it really collected, or counted twice? How much was delivered, and at what moisture? Was the farmer paid? Paper registers and spreadsheets assembled at the end of a year make these questions hard to answer.

How our MRV works

Maya Green records the evidence as the work happens, inside the same app that runs collection and payment.

  • Measure: farm GPS location, the farmer's signed digital agreement and photos, the tractor's GPS trail, and weighbridge weight, moisture and grade at the plant gate.
  • Record: one chain per tonne: farmer → booking → tractor trip → weighbridge → payment. Data-quality flags highlight anything unusual, such as a delivery far heavier than the declared acreage would suggest.
  • Prove: each record carries a SHA-256 fingerprint, and each fingerprint includes the one before it. Change any past record and every fingerprint after it breaks, so edits are visible. Verifiers receive an Excel or PDF export with the full chain.

What a hash chain does, in plain words

Think of each record as a sealed envelope that carries a copy of the seal from the previous envelope. If someone opens and rewrites an old envelope, its seal changes, and the next envelope no longer matches. You do not need to trust whoever holds the files; you can check that the chain is intact.

What this means for buyers and verifiers

  • Fewer conservative deductions: when evidence is complete, verifiers have less reason to discount claims.
  • Lower audit effort: data arrives organised by tonne and by farmer, not reconstructed after the fact.
  • Visible community benefit: every farmer payment is logged with its UPI reference, and the 20% carbon share is tracked per farmer.

What comes next

The farm-to-gate chain is live in our app today. Next, plant data will join the same chain by lot ID: production batches, mass balance from residue to pellets, lab results for moisture and calorific value, and dispatch with the buyer's end-use declaration. Satellite checks for field burning will add independent evidence on avoided burning.

Methodologies for our credits are being confirmed with an experienced Verra consultant, and we claim no credits before validation.
