Transparency · Carbon methodology

How we measure, and what we will not claim

Maya Green · Version 1 · October 2026 · Pre-validation

Carbon credits are only valuable if buyers can trust them. This page explains, in plain words, how Maya Green plans to calculate emission reductions, which data we collect, which deductions we apply, and what we deliberately leave out.

Status: our project is at the pre-feasibility stage. The methodologies below are candidates, to be confirmed with an experienced Verra consultant and validated by an independent auditor (VVB). We will not sell or claim any credit before validation and registry issuance.

1. Our principles

  • Conservative by default: when a value is uncertain, we use the lower estimate of benefit.
  • Measured, not modelled: weights, moisture and distances come from real records, not assumptions.
  • One tonne, one claim: every tonne is tracked from field to buyer so it cannot be counted twice.
  • Independent verification: credits are issued only after third-party validation and verification.
  • Fair share: 20% of net carbon revenue goes back to the farmers who supplied the residue.

3. How a reduction is calculated

In simple form, for each period:

Net reduction = baseline emissions − project emissions − leakage, then minus conservative deductions and a buffer.
  • Baseline: what would have happened without us: coal burned in the buyer's boiler, and for avoided burning, residue burned in the field. For avoided burning we count only residue that the farmer's baseline survey shows was burned in previous seasons.
  • Project emissions: fuel and electricity used to collect, transport and process the residue, including tractor energy, plant electricity and any backup fuel.
  • Leakage: if residue was previously used for something else (for example fodder or household fuel), taking it could push that use elsewhere. Our baseline survey records last season's use so such residue is excluded or adjusted.

5. Deductions we apply

  • Moisture: reductions are calculated on dry weight, not delivered weight.
  • Process loss: a default deduction of 10% for losses between the gate and finished fuel, replaced by measured mass balance once the plant runs.
  • Buffer: 15% of calculated reductions held back for uncertainty, adjusted to the final methodology's requirements.
  • Data-quality flags: deliveries with missing or unusual data are excluded until resolved.

6. What we will not claim

  • No separate credits for our solar power or electric tractors. They lower project emissions, which is already counted.
  • No credits for residue that was not burned or otherwise wasted in the baseline.
  • No coal-displacement credits where the buyer claims the same reduction. Credit ownership is fixed in each supply contract.
  • No credits sold or advertised before validation and issuance by a recognised registry.

7. Data integrity

Every record in our app, from the farmer's signed agreement to the weighbridge entry and the UPI payment, is sealed in a SHA-256 hash chain. Changing any past record breaks every later fingerprint, so edits are visible to auditors. We plan to anchor these fingerprints on a public blockchain so buyers and exchanges can verify them independently. Read more in What is digital MRV.

8. From now to issued credits

  1. Idea note and pre-feasibility with a carbon consultant (now)
  2. Project design document (PDD) and Verra pipeline listing (seed period)
  3. Plant build and independent validation (next round)
  4. Monitoring, verification and issuance (operations)
  5. Sale to buyers through direct contracts and, where suitable, regulated exchanges
